SEED - TrowelTech Plastering, Birmingham

           SECOND BUILD. The first cut (navy panels, orange accent, numbered
           service cards, tick trust strip, a drawn before/after wall) was cut by
           Patrick on review: "the before/after is shit" and "the whole design i
           kinda hate, looks like cheap AI". He picked MATERIAL-LED, REAL SURFACE
           as the replacement identity. Nothing from the first cut survives except
           the logo repair, the copy and the photo research.

Palette:   NO ACCENT COLOUR AT ALL. The only colours on the page are the ones the
           material actually is. White paper, set-plaster stone #ececeb, WET-PLASTER
           INK #17181a doing every panel, button, star and rule, and greys #5a5f63
           and #8f9497 for body and labels. Nothing is tinted, nothing is branded a
           hue. This is the second entry in the whole ledger with no chromatic
           accent (sabr is the other, and that one is a pressure washer's black-and-
           white grime-versus-clean story on a bright page with drawn panels; this
           one is achromatic because the PHOTOGRAPHS carry all the colour and the
           interface deliberately carries none). Argued directly against the last
           ten, which are all "bright page + one hot accent": trs burnt orange, sca
           #ff6600, walnut, board gold, lias red, garden green, delta red, seal
           brass, anthracite and daylight blue, jas2 hi-vis. Also argued against the
           client's own logo: it is an AI-generated navy-and-orange badge, and
           building the palette on it is exactly what produced the cut first cut, so
           the badge is used small in the nav, the loader and the footer and is the
           only colour on the page.
Type:      ONE FAMILY, ONEST, at 400/500/600 (clear of the last ten, next most
           recent use 37 entries back, 5 all-time). A single typeface doing display
           and body, separated only by size, weight and tracking. Deliberate: every
           cut mockup in this style pairs a shouty 900-weight display with a body
           sans, and that pairing is most of the "cheap AI" tell. Nothing here is
           heavier than 600.
Signature: THE WALL IS THE PAGE. The fold is a full-bleed PHOTOGRAPH of a real
           plastered wall with raking window light across it, and the type sets
           directly onto it behind a soft white scrim. The about band and the CTA
           band are full-bleed photographs of real render texture in wet-plaster
           dark. The material is the identity, so the page needs no invented
           graphic device at all. Everything drawn was removed: no brick patterns,
           no CSS walls, no gradients standing in for material.
Texture:   Hairline rules and hard square corners everywhere, zero border-radius
           except the two circular FABs. Service rows, review blocks, contact
           details and the trust strip are all rule-separated rather than boxed:
           no cards, no chips, no pills, no numbered badges, no tick icons.
Loader:    RAKE THE WALL. The fold photograph fills the screen and one hard band
           of light crosses it left to right on a skew, the way a plasterer shines
           a light along a wall to check it for flatness, then the badge and "Flat
           walls, Birmingham" set on top. It is the actual photograph, not a drawn
           stage, which is the whole difference from ld-rakelight (jm-builders-white,
           UNUSED and free: a DRAWN sculpted relief wall built band by band and then
           lit) and from every other lab piece, all of which animate drawn shapes.
           1.55s of movement, teardown at 2.25s, CSS-only fail-safe at 2.6s, JS
           fail-safe at 3.6s, reduced motion drops straight to the badge. Hands off
           into the fold because the loader IS the fold, same photograph, same crop:
           the light finishes crossing and the type sets onto the wall it just lit.
           Patrick's jm-builders-white note is respected: the loader trick is NOT
           repeated as a live band under the hero, it happens once.
Mobile:    The 390 fold is that photograph, full bleed, with the scrim dropped to
           .88 at the top and .1 at the bottom so the surface and the light bars
           are plainly visible behind and below the type. The wow is that the first
           screenful is a real, large, tactile wall rather than a coloured panel,
           and the loader has just raked light across it. Judged at 390 first.
Voice:     David Veitch is off the call sheet and is nowhere public-facing, so no
           owner name is used. CTAs carry the number. No insurance, accreditation
           or years-trading claim is made. The one piece of trade voice on the page
           is the fold's spec line, "Two coats, ruled off, flat to a 2 metre rule",
           which is a real tolerance a plasterer would actually quote.
Ledger ok: Onest clear (37 back), no accent colour to collide with anything, the
           loader is new and is argued against ld-rakelight above, hero+services+
           gallery combo repeats nothing.
