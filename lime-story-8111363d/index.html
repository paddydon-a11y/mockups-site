Featured project

Five years of a garden they couldn’t use.

A failing 2.5m retaining wall in Clayton-le-Woods that nobody else would take on. Structural repair, rebuild, and a garden back in use, with photos from the owners themselves.

When Michael and Heather Tadman bought their house in Clayton-le-Woods, they inherited a problem they didn’t know was coming. A 2.5m high retaining wall at the boundary was cracking and slowly moving, pushed out of line by the roots of a eucalyptus tree that had been taken out long before they moved in. The garden behind it was unusable, and the wall was at real risk of going into next door.

They lived with it for around five years. Builders who didn’t turn up, and on the rare occasion one did, no answer that was affordable, sensible and actually buildable. Then they rang us.

We brought in David Ormes at Partington and Associates Consulting Engineers in Chorley, and between us worked up a design to repair and rebuild the wall cost effectively, without tearing the whole site apart or causing the neighbouring properties more disruption than the job needed. Access was tight, so the dig went in with a micro digger and the spoil came out the same way.

Once the structure was signed off and back in, Ralph at Top Spec Landscaping finished it with new turf and Indian sandstone paving. This summer, for the first time since they moved in, Michael and Heather have a garden to be proud of and somewhere to sit out on a warm evening.

Every photo here is theirs, shared from their own social media posts.

On site now

A cottage in Midge Hall, almost 50% bigger.

A single-storey side extension to a mews cottage near Leyland. Groundworks are under way and we expect to be finished on site in around ten weeks.

Gill and Kevin have been thinking about this project for many years, and we are pleased to have been able to help them budget, design, plan and build it exactly as they wanted.

It is a small job, and one we should have started some months ago. After a holiday abroad, Kevin was taken seriously ill, and everything was rightly put on hold. Thankfully he has made a full recovery, and after some works to the existing part of the house, we have now started on the new build extension.

The extension gives the cottage almost 50% more living space: a new bedroom or snug under a vaulted ceiling, a shower room and a utility, with rendered walls and a slate roof to match the original house. Our groundworkers have started on the foundations and slab, and we hope to be finished on site in around ten weeks.

With the extension under way, Gill and Kevin already have their eyes on updating the kitchen and other rooms in the house next. We will share more photos as the build goes up.
